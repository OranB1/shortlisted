# UK Specialty Training Data — v3: CST, O&G, Radiology, Ophthalmology, Paediatrics & Prioritisation Policy

*Third file in the series. Combines the official research audit (v3-research), user-supplied official documents (RCSI CST intake report, RCPCH Paediatrics glossary), and additional official pages fetched directly. Read alongside `UK_Specialty_Training_Data_Compendium_v2.md` (IMT deep-dive) and `All_Specialty_Competition_Ratios.md` (all-specialty ratios).*

## TL;DR

- **CST now has a full, official, IMT-equivalent scoring matrix** — verbatim from NHS England, current for 2025/26, with an already-flagged 2027 restructure to Good Medical Practice domains.
- **A genuinely new, official CST dataset has emerged: the RCSI Core Surgical Training Intake Report 2020–2025.** This is Ireland-specific (RCSI/HSE/NDTP, not UK NHS England), but gives exactly the shortlist/interview-funnel numbers that were missing for UK CST — applications, interview call rates, offers, and a **genuine year-by-year quality threshold ("Below the Desired Quality Standard")** figure. Useful as a structural analogue even though it's a different jurisdiction; do not present Irish CST numbers as UK CST numbers in the app.
- **Paediatrics ST1 now has its full official 5-domain shortlisting scoring glossary**, verbatim, including scoring-glossary definitions for every term assessors use — the most detailed and explicit specialty document seen yet, arguably a template for how the app should explain scoring to users.
- **The single most important finding this pass: the Medical Training (Prioritisation) Act 2026.** This is now live law (Royal Assent 6 March 2026) and applies to every specialty except ST1 Public Health, for every 2026 recruitment round. It fundamentally changes how a likelihood model must work — see Part 6 below. This is not optional context; it changes ranking mechanics for essentially the entire product.
- O&G's IDT (inter-deanery transfer) statistics were pulled but are a different dataset than initial ST1 recruitment — see caveat in Part 3.

## Key Findings

1. **CST portfolio scoring (official, verbatim, current for 2025/26):** five domains — Operative Experience (A–E by case count 40+/30–39/20–29/11–19/≤10), Surgical Experience (binary: taster/elective or not), QI/Audit (A–E by leadership level, plus a *separate* presentation sub-score A–C), Presentations & Publications (A–E by authorship/prize tier), Teaching Experience (A–E by design/delivery level). Full evidence requirements, exemplar documents, and a probity warning are all specified. **Portfolio is now assessed live at interview (a 2025/26 change), not pre-scored** — assessors get 10–15 minutes with uploaded evidence, then ask questions on two assessor-chosen domains.
2. **A 2027 CST restructure is already flagged**: domains will be renamed/regrouped to match GMC Good Medical Practice categories (Knowledge & Skills; Safety & Culture; Communication) — a "cosmetic" change per NHS England's own description, but worth tracking for when the app needs updating.
3. **The Irish RCSI CST report gives what UK CST lacks**: a real, published, year-by-year "quality standard" cutoff. In 2025, RCSI **lowered its cutoff to appoint 100 trainees after decliners** — 114 candidates were rated "below the desired quality standard" out of 214 interviewed, meaning genuine appointability sits meaningfully below the interview-invite line. Applications to RCSI CST also jumped dramatically: 304→487 (2024→2025), a 60% single-year increase, sharper than any UK specialty tracked.
4. **Paediatrics scoring glossary is a gold-standard reference document** — it defines exactly what "local," "regional," "national," "distinctly different," "designed," "led," and "good quality" mean for scoring purposes, removing ambiguity that most other specialties leave applicants to guess at.
5. **The Prioritisation Act reshapes the entire ranking model.** From 2026, most applicants are split into two priority tiers before rank ever matters, and only when the priority tier is exhausted does the non-priority tier get considered — full mechanics in Part 6.

## Details

### PART 1 — Core Surgical Training (CST), UK: full official scoring matrix

Source: NHS England, "Core Surgical Training Portfolio Guidance for Candidates," https://medical.hee.nhs.uk/medical-training-recruitment/medical-specialty-training/surgery/core-surgery/core-surgical-training-portfolio-guidance-for-candidates (page last reviewed 23 October 2025, next review 23 October 2027).

**Structural change for 2025/26**: portfolio is no longer scored as a standalone pre-interview self-assessment. It is assessed live as an interview station — candidates upload an index sheet + evidence, assessors get 10–15 minutes to review, then ask questions on **two assessor-chosen domains** (not candidate-chosen). Missing index sheet = automatic 0. Only the first item of evidence per domain is assessed if multiple are uploaded.

**The five domains, in full:**

1. **Operative Experience** (via verified eLogbook consolidation report, one combined report only): A = 40+ cases; B = 30–39; C = 20–29; D = 11–19; E = ≤10/no evidence.
2. **Surgical Experience**: A = surgical taster week (min 5 days, non-consecutive allowed) OR surgical elective (min 4 weeks); B = neither. **Foundation placements no longer count** (2025/26 change — pandemic-era allowance withdrawn). **ITU no longer counts as a surgical specialty** (2025/26 change).
3. **Quality Improvement/Clinical Audit**: two sub-scores.
   - Leadership level: A = led ALL aspects of a *surgically-themed* audit/QI with demonstrated change (2+ cycles); B = led ALL aspects of a non-surgical audit/QI with change; C = contributor through multiple cycles; D = involved in one cycle (e.g. data collection); E = none/other.
   - Presentation (separately scored, only if A/B/C above): A = presented both cycles OR the intervention+change; B = presented one cycle; C = did not present. **2025/26 change**: panellists now expect the presentation to have occurred *in the area intended to bring about change* — e.g. a compliance audit's first cycle must be presented locally, since that's part of the change process.
4. **Presentations and Publications**: A = won top prize for an invited/selected oral presentation at national/international meeting, OR first author of a PubMed-cited publication (not case report/editorial); B = delivered an oral presentation after selection, OR first-author prize-winning/oral poster, OR first-author PubMed case report/editorial/book chapter; C = named co-author of one PubMed-cited publication; D = first-author poster, OR invited regional oral presentation, OR cited collaborative author; E = none/other. Requires PubMed ID for all print publications — no ID, 0 points, and a false PubMed claim risks a probity referral to the GMC.
5. **Teaching Experience**: A = designed/organised a face-to-face programme (4+ sessions) AND delivered 4+; B = same but online; C = designed/organised but delivered <4; D = regular teaching (4+/year) OR a "learning to teach" course; E = none. Formal feedback evidence mandatory for all levels.

**Evidence rules that apply across all domains**: electronic signatures are scrutinised for tampering; supervisor GMC numbers are mandatory (missing = 0); patient-identifiable data anywhere in evidence = 0 for that domain plus potential GMC referral; a single achievement can only be claimed in one domain, in its highest-scoring category.

**Confirmed upcoming change (2027)**: domains will be relabelled to match GMC Good Medical Practice categories — Knowledge & Skills (≈ Surgical Experience), Safety & Culture (≈ QI/Audit), Communication (≈ Publications/Presentations/Teaching, broadened to include other communication modalities). NHS England describes this as "a cosmetic change," implying scoring logic will map across rather than being rebuilt from scratch — worth confirming again nearer 2027.

**No official UK CST score distribution exists** (confirmed again this pass) — iscp.ac.uk and jcie.org.uk still do not publish shortlist/total score distributions.

### PART 2 — CST Ireland (RCSI): a real analogue dataset, NOT UK data

Source: RCSI Surgical Affairs, "Core Surgical Training Intake Report 2020–2025," June 2025 (user-supplied PDF; document owner RCSI/HSE/NDTP, approved by the Irish Postgraduate Training Committee of RCSI — ISPTC).

**This is Ireland's Core Surgical Training scheme, administered by RCSI/HSE, entirely separate from UK NHS England CST.** It is included here because it's the only place a genuine "quality threshold" concept for core surgical training could be found publicly, and the funnel structure (applicants → called for interview → interviewed → below quality standard → offers) is a useful template even if the numbers themselves are Irish, not UK.

**CST Ireland intake summary, 2020–2025:**

| Year | Applicants | Called for interview | Interviewed | First round offers | Below quality standard | Accepted 1st round |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2020 | 162 | 135 | 130 | 60 | 0 | 59 |
| 2021 | 250 | 232 | 212 | 80 | 142 | 77 |
| 2022 | 286 | 244 | 228 | 79 | 138 | 77 |
| 2023 | 263 | 229 | 215 | 80 | 122 | 76 |
| 2024 | 304 | 217 | 200 | 79 | 123 | 77 |
| 2025 | 487 | 228 | 214 | 100 | 114* | 98 |

*2025 footnote: "Lowered cutoff in 2025 to appoint 100 trainees after declinations" — i.e. RCSI deliberately relaxed the quality bar to fill the intake after enough candidates turned down offers, a genuinely useful real-world example of how "quality standard" thresholds flex against fill-rate pressure.

**Other RCSI CST data points**: mean applicant age has risen from 27.0 (2020) to 29.3 (2025 application) / 27.9 (2025 offer); MRCS Part A pass-rate-at-application has risen sharply (23% of 2020 applicants had Part A → 37% of 2025 applicants); gender split has stayed roughly 60:40 male:female at both application and offer stage across all years; a large and growing share of applicants are international (Sudan, Pakistan, and Malaysia are the largest non-Ireland/non-UK source countries, with Pakistan rising from 2 applicants in 2020 to 99 in 2025 and Sudan from 3 to 52).

**Do not present this as UK CST data in the app** — flag it explicitly as "Ireland (RCSI), included as a structural reference" if used at all, given the risk of a user assuming it reflects the NHS England process they're actually applying to.

### PART 3 — O&G, Radiology, Ophthalmology: inter-deanery transfer (IDT) data — a different dataset than initial recruitment

Source: NHS England IDT statistics pages (O&G: .../inter-deanery-transfers-idt/statistics/top-6/obstetrics-and-gynaecology-statistics; equivalent pages exist for Radiology and Ophthalmology at the same path pattern).

**Important distinction**: this data is about **existing trainees applying to transfer between deaneries mid-training** (e.g. an ST3 O&G trainee in Severn wants to move to North London) — it is NOT about the initial ST1 competition data covered in Part 3/6 of the v3-research file. It exists twice yearly (February and August rounds) back to 2013, broken down by region, showing applications-to-join, eligible-applications, and offers-made per region per round.

**What this data is useful for**: showing which regions are hardest to *transfer into* later in training (a genuinely different, later-career question than "where's easiest to get an ST1 offer"). North London and South London consistently show the highest IDT application volumes for O&G (18–20 applications per round in recent years vs. 0–7 for most other regions), suggesting London remains the most sought-after O&G region at both entry and mid-training stages — consistent with the IMT regional-competitiveness pattern found earlier.

**Radiology IDT (fetched, official)**: same pattern, even more pronounced. North London alone drew 32 applications-to-join in August 2024 (vs. single digits or zero for most other regions) and 26 in February 2024 — consistently 3–6× the volume of the next-busiest region (South London). North London/South London combined regularly account for 40–50% of all national IDT demand for Radiology. Total national IDT volume for Radiology has grown steadily: from ~10–20 applications per round pre-2020 to 50–69 per round in 2023–2025.

**Ophthalmology IDT (fetched, official) — a genuinely different, notable pattern**: Ophthalmology has essentially no IDT data. It only qualified as a "top 6" IDT specialty (i.e. had enough volume to be tracked) for a single round — August 2021 (15 total applications, 7 offers) — and NHS England's page explicitly states it was **not** a top-6 specialty for every round since (Feb 2022 through Feb 2025). This most likely reflects Ophthalmology's much smaller total trainee cohort rather than unusually high satisfaction/low desire to move, but it does mean: **there is no usable regional-transfer signal for Ophthalmology**, unlike O&G and Radiology. Note also this specific page is stale — last reviewed 2 January 2024, next review was due 2 January 2025 (overdue) — so it's possible more recent data exists that hasn't been published yet; worth a periodic re-check.

**RCOG Training Data Analysis 2025 (fetched)**: this turned out to be the RCOG's **Training Evaluation Form (TEF)** — an annual trainee-experience survey — not recruitment/scoring data. It contains reports on Subspecialty Training, Special Interest Training, Differential Attainment, Educational Supervision, Gynaecological Training, Obstetric Training, Wellbeing & Burnout, and Workplace Behaviours. None of this is directly usable for the scoring/likelihood engine, but two findings are worth flagging as context if Shortlisted ever builds trust/culture-related features: (1) the 2025 Differential Attainment report notes a growing international-medical-graduate and ethnic-minority share of the O&G trainee workforce; (2) the Workplace Behaviours report found International Medical Graduates over twice as likely to report bullying/undermining as UK graduates (21.4% vs 10%) — a real equity signal, though not a recruitment-scoring one.

### PART 4 — Paediatrics ST1: full official shortlisting scoring glossary (verbatim)

Source: RCPCH, "ST1 Scoring Guidance & Glossary for Shortlisting 2026-27" v.4 (user-supplied PDF, updated 27 October 2025) and RCPCH ST1 application page (rcpch.ac.uk/education-careers/apply-paediatrics/ST1, last modified 28 October 2025).

**Five domains, 30 points total per assessor (two assessors score independently; scores combined for shortlisting rank only — does not carry to final interview rank):**

1. **Transferable clinical capabilities** (out of 8): up to five examples with reflections. Scored 0 (no evidence) through 8 (five distinctly different, clearly relevant examples with reflection), in a defined ladder. Max 2 of the 5 examples can be practical clinical procedures (cannula, venepuncture, LP, NG tube, airway maintenance).
2. **Personal achievements and reflection** (out of 8): same 0–8 ladder structure, for achievements distinct from Section 1's examples — medical or non-medical, community/charity work counts if clearly linked to suitability for paediatrics.
3. **QIP/Audit** (out of 5): 0 (none) → 5 (planned/coordinated AND presented a good-quality project at regional/national/international level with results/recommendations described). Best of up to 2 examples counted, not cumulative.
4. **Academic achievements** (out of 3, +1 bonus): 0 (no detail) → 3 (detailed description with national/international presentation OR first/last-author publication). **+1 additional point for a genuine research qualification** (PG Cert, PG Dip, MSc, MD via original research ≥2 years, PhD) — but this bonus does NOT automatically produce a 4; it's added on top of whatever the base 0–3 score is. **28 October 2025 update**: research qualifications must now be named explicitly in the final text-box ("Describe the nature of your research...").
5. **Teaching/Education** (out of 5): 0 (none) → 5 (designed, delivered, led, evaluated, AND used feedback to improve future sessions).

**Full official glossary of scoring terms** (verbatim definitions provided): distinguishes "no relevance" vs "minimal relevance" (generic enough to apply to any specialty = minimal), defines "distinctly different" examples (near-duplicate examples cap the achievable score), and gives worked local/regional/national/international definitions consistent with the CST document's definitions (local = within one Trust/hospital; regional = across a deanery/ICB/multi-site network; national = a UK-wide organising body like RCPCH or BMA; international = a genuinely international organising body).

**Structural notes**: no MSRA. Shortlisting score is a gate only — does not carry into final rank. Final rank/appointability comes entirely from the two-station virtual interview (Qpercom platform, 20 min/station, ~45 min total; domains: Communication, Career Motivation, Clinical Reasoning, Reflective Practice), appointability threshold 55%.

**2026-27 confirmed structural changes** (from the RCPCH page, distinct from the glossary PDF itself): the two clinical sections were "refined for greater distinction," and the **Additional Qualifications section has been removed entirely** (previously existed as a separate scored section in 2025-26 — folded out for 2026-27, reducing from 6 scored sections to 5).

**2026-27 competition and post numbers (official, RCPCH)**: 2,780 applications for 476 posts in 2025-26 (up from 1,583 applications in 2024-25) — ratio 5.63, fill rate 99.57% (474/476 filled). Indicative regional post ranges for 2026-27 (min–max, to be finalised nearer preferencing): London 105–115, Yorkshire & Humber 45–48, East of England 35–40, West Midlands 28–29, Kent/Surrey/Sussex 18–32, Scotland 19–24, Wales 14–19, East Midlands 12–35, North East 15–20, Thames Valley 16–16 (fixed), Wessex 16–16 (fixed), Northern Ireland 10–17, North West 0–22, North West Mersey 0–20, SW Peninsula 1–11, SW Severn 1–15. RCPCH explicitly states a **true regional competition ratio cannot be calculated** since applicants can preference any/all regions simultaneously.

### PART 6 — THE MEDICAL TRAINING (PRIORITISATION) ACT 2026 — critical, changes the entire ranking model

Source: NHS England, "Prioritisation for Specialty Recruitment," https://medical.hee.nhs.uk/medical-training-recruitment/medical-specialty-training/prioritisation-for-specialty-recruitment (page last reviewed 16 June 2026). Underlying legislation: Medical Training (Prioritisation) Act 2026, Royal Assent **6 March 2026**, in force for **all 2026 recruitment rounds** (including 2026 Round 3, which appoints for 2027-start posts) and applies to **every specialty except ST1 Public Health**.

**This is not a minor policy footnote — it restructures how offers are actually made, and any "likelihood of getting an offer" feature in the app is currently incomplete without it.**

**How it works:**
- Every applicant is sorted into exactly one of two groups before offers begin: **"in the priority group"** or **"not in the priority group."** There is no ranking within a group — everyone in the priority group has equal priority regardless of score.
- **Priority is determined by three factors together**: (1) country of primary medical qualification (UK, Ireland, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, or Switzerland — with a further test excluding people who did the *majority* of that training outside the British Isles/Ireland); (2) immigration status (British citizen, Commonwealth citizen with right of abode, Irish citizen, Indefinite Leave to Remain/Settled Status, EU settled or pre-settled status — explicitly **excluding** Partner/Dependent visa holders); (3) having completed or currently being on a "relevant qualifying UK programme" — for CT1/ST1 this means the **UK Foundation Programme only**; for ST3/ST4 it extends to LAT posts in the *same* specialty and other relevant UK training programmes that are recognised entry routes for that specialty (e.g. IMT counts as qualifying for Cardiology ST4 but not for Urology ST3).
- **Explicitly NOT qualifying programmes**: the Malta Foundation Programme, Trust-based (non-HEE) programmes, and non-training posts leading to an Alternative Certificate of Eligibility.
- **Crucially: rank itself is unaffected by priority status.** "Prioritisation does not affect the rank applicants receive following interview; ranks are based on performance in the selection process only." What changes is the **order in which groups are considered for offers**: the entire priority group (in rank order) is worked through first; only once every appointable priority-group candidate has been offered/declined does the system move to the non-priority group, again in rank order.
- Non-priority applicants who are appointable and invited to interview should still attend and can still receive an offer — but only after the priority group is exhausted, and only if posts remain.
- For 2026, immigration status is being used as a **practical proxy** for "has significant NHS/UK healthcare system experience." From 2027, DHSC gains power to define a further prioritised group directly by NHS experience rather than immigration status alone — not yet determined, expected to be published autumn 2026 ahead of 2027 recruitment.
- Applicants are told their priority status before offers begin each round and can request a review before offers commence for that round (deadlines are tight — e.g. for 2027 Round 1, applications close 19 Nov 2026 and the review-request deadline is 24 Nov 2026, 17:00). Reviews are not possible once offers have started, even for specialties where offers haven't begun yet.

**What this means for the Shortlisted likelihood model:**
- A raw score/rank-based likelihood estimate will systematically **overstate the chances of non-priority applicants** (most commonly: international medical graduates without ILR/settled status, or those on Partner/Dependent visas) and will need a second, explicit "priority status" input alongside the portfolio/MSRA score.
- Two applicants with an identical score/rank can have meaningfully different real-world odds purely based on where they qualified and their immigration status — this is now a first-class variable, not a footnote.
- This also likely explains part of the sharp 2024→2025 competition ratio rises seen across nearly every specialty (more applicants competing for the same non-priority "leftover" pool, on top of raw application growth) — worth flagging as a contributing factor when presenting historic ratio trends, though the Act itself only took effect for 2026 recruitment, so 2025 ratios predate it.

## Recommendations

1. **Add "Prioritisation Status" as a first-class input to the scoring/likelihood engine** — ask users about primary medical qualification country, immigration status, and Foundation Programme (or LAT/qualifying programme for ST3/4) completion, and split the likelihood output by priority-group membership rather than presenting one blended number.
2. **Populate CST's scoring page with full confidence** — it's official, current, and structured exactly like IMT's, so it can go straight into the app's specialty-scoring database.
3. **Keep the RCSI CST Ireland data clearly labelled as a separate, non-UK reference dataset** if it's used at all — the "quality standard" concept is a good structural idea to borrow for how the app frames appointability, but the numbers themselves must never be shown as if they're NHS England figures.
4. **Paediatrics' scoring glossary is worth using as a template** for how Shortlisted explains scoring criteria to users generally — its definitions of "distinctly different," "local/regional/national," etc. are clearer than most other specialties' guidance and could inform a shared glossary feature across all specialties in the app, not just Paediatrics.
5. **Radiology's IDT pattern reinforces the CST/O&G regional finding** — build "London is disproportionately competitive" as a general cross-specialty assumption in the app's regional-likelihood layer, not something to re-derive per specialty. **Ophthalmology is the exception**: its trainee pool is too small to generate a usable regional-transfer signal, so don't force a regional-difficulty feature onto Ophthalmology using this method — its regional data will need to come from the Evidence Folder/interview scoring pattern instead, if anywhere.

## Caveats
- The RCSI CST report is Ireland-specific (RCSI/HSE/NDTP), not NHS England — a different training system entirely, included only as a useful structural analogue.
- IDT statistics (O&G, and the not-yet-fetched Radiology/Ophthalmology equivalents) measure trainee transfers between deaneries mid-training, not initial ST1 recruitment competition — don't conflate the two datasets.
- Radiology Cafe was not fetched this pass; treat any Radiology competition-ratio claim from it as secondary until cross-checked against the official NHS England competition-ratios page already on file.
- The Prioritisation Act's effects on competition ratios can only be observed from 2026 recruitment data onward — all historic ratios in prior files (2013–2025) predate the Act and should not be assumed to reflect the same competitive dynamics going forward.
- Page review dates: CST portfolio guidance reviewed 23 Oct 2025 (next due 23 Oct 2027); O&G IDT stats reviewed 30 Jan 2026 (next due 30 Jan 2028); Prioritisation policy page reviewed 16 June 2026 (next due 16 June 2027) — re-verify against these pages before each new recruitment cycle, particularly the Prioritisation policy given DHSC's 2027 NHS-experience criteria are still to be published.
