## Questions prepared for the call

- How long does it typically take from idea to approval for an audit or QIP to actually run?
- How many audits/QIPs do departments/doctors sit on that aren't being done due to being too busy/lack of staff?
- "Walk me through what happens today when a consultant has a QI idea, from the idea to a doctor or student actually being assigned it — where does it get logged, and where does it usually get stuck?"
- Where do most of C&W's QIP ideas actually come from — individual trainees bringing their own ideas, or the QI team/department already having a backlog of things they want improved and needing hands to run them? (Given Jemma's role, the second is plausible and arguably more likely for QI specifically vs. research, which tends to be more student/academic-driven — this changes the pitch from "help students find opportunities" to "give your team a fast way to staff the backlog you already have.")

## Evidence prepared — where the student fits

- Med student-led QI projects achieved significant improvement in their primary outcome in 50% of cases, and all of those improvements were sustained at follow-up.
- A paper on core medical training recruitment found that giving students well-supervised QI projects can help make a specialty more attractive and address recruitment shortfalls.

## Evidence prepared — staff capacity/completion problem

BMA survey of UK doctors:
- 67% wanted to make improvements in the past year
- 89% faced barriers doing so
- Of those with obstacles, 52% cited lack of time and capacity as the biggest barrier
- Only 16.7% of physicians had any designated time for QI work built into their schedule; when time was designated, 86.6% reported participation

Study of Foundation doctors:
- 73.7% of F1s and 65.7% of F2s attempted an audit, but only 30.2% of F1 audits and 58.5% of F2 audits were actually completed
- 57.9% of F1s failed to complete an audit entirely
- Similar pattern even among senior paediatric trainees at registrar level: audit activity above 90%, but only 16% completed the full audit cycle

**Headline framing:** the problem isn't a lack of appetite or ideas — appetite is consistently high (67–86% depending on the study). It's that projects start and then stall or die before completion, at every level of seniority.

## Evidence prepared — access inequality angle

- Practical, cultural, and political barriers restrict students from research and audit work — perceived lack of opportunity and hostile environments being the main barriers.
- Students with fewer existing contacts in medicine have less access to opportunities.
- A study found socioeconomic and demographic background predicts self-reported familiarity with postgrad training pathways — some students understand "the game" earlier than others purely because of who they know.

SMART survey (1,774 medical students across 40 UK medical schools):
- 68.0% cited limited awareness of opportunities as a barrier to research engagement, and 67.5% cited time constraints — awareness of what's even available is the single biggest blocker, ahead of time.
- 69.16% wanted to do more research in future; 59.54% found it useful to do research at medical school — appetite is high even where access isn't.
- 46.67% felt there were barriers actively preventing them from doing research; 73.49% found it at least somewhat difficult to combine research with medical school.

**Framing used for "equalising the playing field":** a structured, visible platform diminishes the advantage that outgoing, well-networked students currently have simply from being better at approaching consultants directly. It also undercuts a bad dynamic that's emerged around this: students paying private companies thousands of pounds for "research opportunities" because they can't access any through normal channels.

## Suggested call structure (as prepped)

1. Open by asking about her role and remit, and how QIPs currently move through C&W. Let her talk first.
2. Ask what "success" looks like for the QI team — completion rate, projects/year, publications.
3. Ask the process questions above — how ideas get logged, how long approval takes, roughly how many projects sit undone due to capacity.
4. Introduce the platform as a response to the completion-collapse stats (not the inequality angle, which is a softer statistical claim, better as a secondary point).
5. Ask directly how a pilot could plug into what they already do — RIQI portal, Clinical Governance approval, what she'd need to see to trust student-sourced applicants with a live project.
6. Ask what would go wrong — governance, patient data/confidentiality, supervision capacity, anything that's killed similar ideas before.
7. Close with a small concrete next step (e.g. running it with two or three willing consultants first) rather than asking for a Trust-wide rollout decision on a first call.

## Supporting evidence gathered but not necessarily used on the call

- QI skills are transferable to research; some specialties specifically require a QI project in that specialty (not just any QI).
- In a review of student QI project impact, 5 of 6 articles' recommendations had been implemented as part of health service delivery.
- Over 50% of medical students report inadequate exposure to QI at med school despite recognising its importance.
- Imperial runs first-year QI teaching including a formally assessed 10-week group project under physician guidance.
- At Dundee, a Student Selected Component option is a 4-week QI project via the IHI Open School Practicum — lesson from that programme: framing a project as "improving local practice" gets far more staff buy-in than framing it as "a student project."
- Batalden and Davidoff: "healthcare will not realise its full potential unless change making becomes an intrinsic part of everyone's job, every day, in all parts of the system."
- NHS records ~2.4 million patient safety incidents a year (~30% some harm, ~12,000 severe harm/death); medication errors alone estimated to cost the NHS up to £2.5bn/year — the scale of the problem QI work exists to chip away at.
- Cross-trust comparison: because the platform would naturally stratify projects by trust and region, it could let a trust see what QI/audit work is happening elsewhere — inspiration for their own projects, and a route into cross-trust collaboration that doesn't really exist in an accessible form today. Flagged in the prep notes as a longer-term feature, not a pilot promise — and independently raised by Jemma herself on the call as something C&W already wants (see transcript).
