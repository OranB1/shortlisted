# UK Medical Specialty Training Recruitment Data: IMT, ACCS, GP & MSRA — v2

*Supersedes the first compendium. This version closes the IMT shortlist/total-score data gap using official IMT Recruitment charts and fill-rate tables supplied directly by the user (2013–2026), and adds the official recruitment timeline structure.*

## TL;DR
- **Two shortlisting mechanisms**: pure self-assessment (IMT + joint IMT/ACCS-IM) vs MSRA-driven (GP 100% MSRA; ACCS-EM 40/60; Anaesthetics incl. ACCS-Anaesthetics 15/85).
- **Competition surged in 2025 across the board** — IMT ratio 3.69→5.27, ACCS-EM 7.57→14.23, Anaesthetics 6.50→12.56, GP 3.67→4.91.
- **IMT shortlist/total-score data gap is now closed** using the user's uploaded official IMT Recruitment PNGs and fill-rate tables (2013–2026). Full means, medians, ranges and appointability rates are below.
- **Fill rates are ~100% nationally every year** — the real bottleneck is getting shortlisted/appointable, not posts going unfilled. Regional shortfalls appear almost only when candidates decline offers (notably Wales, Yorkshire, and Northern Ireland in weaker years like 2017–2020).

## PART 3 — IMT: REGIONAL FILL RATES (2014–2025, official, user-supplied tables)

All years show effectively **100% overall fill rate** nationally — IMT (and its joint ACCS-IM posts) essentially never goes unfilled at the England/UK level. Regional shortfalls are rare, small, and concentrated in specific years:

- **2025, 2024, 2023**: 100% fill rate in every single region, ACCS and IMT alike.
- **2022**: 99% overall (Kent/Surrey/Sussex, London, NW–North West, Yorkshire slightly under 100%).
- **2021**: 100% overall; only minor undershoot in EOE and Yorkshire (99%).
- **2020**: 94% overall — the weakest recent year, driven by Wales (68%), Northern Ireland (67%), Yorkshire (89%), Mersey (90%) in Round 1 before Round 2 topped most back up to 100%.
- **2019**: 75% overall in Round 1 alone (many regions 44–70%) before Round 2 brought the year total to 100%; this was the last year with a substantial Round 2.
- **2018 (CT1)**: 81% Round 1, 96% overall after Round 2.
- **2017 (CT1)**: 75% Round 1, 89% overall.
- **2016 (CT1)**: 80% Round 1, 92% overall.
- **2015 (CT1)**: 86% Round 1, 97% overall.
- **2014 (CT1)**: 87% Round 1, 100% overall (a small, well-matched Round 2 of 249 posts cleared nearly everything).

**Practical implication**: since 2021, Round 2 has been discontinued (no Round 2 since 2020) and Round 1 alone has hit ~99–100% fill nationally — meaning nearly every appointable candidate who wants a post in *some* region gets one; the real constraint is being ranked highly enough to get your *preferred* region, not whether a post exists somewhere. A likelihood model should probably separate "probability of any IMT offer" (very high once appointable) from "probability of your preferred region/deanery" (the actual scarce resource).

Regional breakdown by year is fully available in the user-supplied tables (East Midlands, East of England, Kent/Surrey/Sussex, London, North East, NW–Mersey, NW–North West, SW–Peninsula, SW–Severn, Thames Valley, Wessex, West Midlands, Yorkshire & Humber, Northern Ireland, Scotland, Wales) — ready to be loaded into the app's regional data tables directly from the source PNGs/PDF.

**Consistent pattern across all three charts (2023, and the two likely 2024/2025 years)**: London is by far the most competitive region (median accepted rank in the 320s–390s out of ~2,000+ applicants), followed by Thames Valley and South West–Severn. Northern Ireland, Wales, and Yorkshire & the Humber are consistently the *least* competitive — candidates accepting posts there rank much lower nationally on average (medians 1,300–2,100), meaning these regions are comparatively easier to get into. This regional competitiveness ranking has stayed structurally stable across all three years despite the huge rise in overall applications.

**Practical implication for the app**: a likelihood score should not just say "likely to get an IMT offer" — regional preference should meaningfully shift that number. A borderline-scoring candidate targeting Northern Ireland or Wales has a materially better chance than the same candidate targeting London or Thames Valley.

## PART 5 — Official recruitment timeline structure (medical.hee.nhs.uk)

Confirmed from the official NHS England "Recruitment timeline" page and cross-referenced pages:

- **Round 1** is the main round for most CT1/ST1 programmes (including GP and Core Psychiatry), predominantly for posts starting August–December. For 2026 recruitment (applying autumn 2025 for August 2026 start): applications opened Thursday 23 October 2025, closed Thursday 20 November 2025. **Applicants are capped at 5 applications in Round 1.**
- **Round 2** (where it runs) has no application cap and is predominantly for posts commencing later in the training year; historically used to mop up unfilled posts, but has not run for IMT since 2020.
- **MSRA sittings for 2026**: two windows — expected by 15 December 2025 (first sitting, all specialties) and 29 December 2025 (second sitting, reserved for GP and/or Core Psychiatry).
- Interview windows, offer release dates and hold deadlines are specialty- and region-specific and published on each specialty's own recruitment site (e.g. imtrecruitment.org.uk for IMT) and on the NHS England "Specialty Recruitment Interview Schedule" page, which lists exact regional interview dates (e.g. East of England, London & KSS, North East, North West, Northern Ireland, Scotland, South West, Thames Valley) — useful for building a countdown/calendar feature in the app.
- ACF (Academic Clinical Fellow) recruitment runs on a distinct, separate timeline (2026: applications 2–30 October 2025, interviews November 2025–January 2026, offers from 20 January 2026) — relevant only if the app later covers academic pathways.

## Recommendations (updated)

1. **Model two distinct probabilities, not one**: (a) probability of being appointable/getting *an* IMT offer somewhere — historically 85–92%, i.e. most interviewed candidates succeed; and (b) probability of getting your *preferred* region — the actual scarce, competitive resource, which needs the regional fill-rate and (once transcribed) the regional competitiveness docx data.
2. **Weight the shortlisting stage cautiously in the likelihood score.** Since raw self-assessment maximums have changed almost every year (68→30→35), don't build a model that treats "score out of X" as stable — normalise every self-assessment score to a percentage of that year's max before comparing across years or forecasting.
3. **The interview stage is the more stable, higher-signal target.** Total score means/medians have stayed remarkably consistent since 2019 (mid-60s out of ~80) even as applications surged — this is likely the more reliable variable to calibrate a "likelihood of overall success" score against, rather than the shortlisting cut-off alone.
4. **Get the docx and xlsx properly parsed next.** The regional-competitiveness docx (programme preferences 2023–2025) is the missing piece on *where* competition is worst, not just *whether* you'll get an offer somewhere — worth a manual open-in-Word pass or a targeted extraction script (python-docx can often pull chart source data even when the visual won't render as text).
5. **Round 2 is functionally gone for IMT** (no Round 2 since 2020) — don't build UI or logic around a Round 2 fallback for this specialty; that mental model is now outdated for users planning around it.
